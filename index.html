    How to use a coupon at checkout

    Works for every deal on this page — online, pickup, or in store.

    1. Clip or reveal the code first.Tap “Get Coupon” on the store you want — the code is copied and saved to your Coupon Wallet.

    Parts fitment

    How to make sure a part actually fits

    The step that saves you from buying an expensive paperweight.

    1. Find your exact model number.It's on a label on the device itself — inside a fridge or washer door, under a laptop, on the back of an appliance — not just the brand name.
    2. Match the compatibility note.Every part comparison here shows what it fits. Match model, size, and connector (for example USB-C 65W, or a 16x25x1 filter) — all three, not just one.
    3. Beware the look-alike generation.Similar names often mean different parts — a radio built for a W209 CLK does not fit a W208, even though both are “2003-era CLK”. When in doubt, search by chassis or generation, not just year.
    4. Re-check on the store page.Enter your model in the store's fit checker before paying, and keep the box until you've test-fitted the part.
    Worked example

    How to replace the radio in a 2003 Mercedes-Benz CLK 430 (W208)

    The parts search on the listings page, finished: head unit + install kit, about an hour with basic tools.

    1. Buy the install parts with the radio.You need a single-DIN dash kit, a wiring-harness adapter, and an antenna adapter for the W208 — sold as bundles in the parts listings. Don't cut the factory harness.
    2. Disconnect the battery first.Negative terminal off before you touch any wiring — the radio circuit is live with the key out.
    3. Release the factory radio.Slide the Mercedes radio-removal keys into the slots on the factory unit until they click, then pull the unit straight out and unplug the connectors at the back.
    4. Wire the new radio on the bench.Match the harness adapter to the new radio's harness color-for-color (power, ground, four speaker pairs), crimp or solder each joint, then plug the adapter into the car — no factory wires cut.
    5. Check for the fiber-optic catch.Many W208s with the Bose system send audio over a fiber-optic (D2B) loop, so an aftermarket radio can't drive the factory amp directly. If yours has Bose, plan on a fiber-loop workaround or rewiring the speakers to the new head unit before you button up.
    6. Test before you reassemble.Reconnect the battery, power the radio on, and check every speaker, the antenna, and steering-wheel controls if fitted. Only then slide the unit into the dash kit until it locks.
    Project guide

    How to build your first model airplane kit

    Kit + the misc parts that finish it — the same split the search shows you.

    1. Pick the right first kit.Start with a beginner balsa kit or an RC trainer kit (a high-wing Cub-style design is the forgiving classic). Save warbirds and big scale kits for build two.
    2. Lay out every part before glue.Check the parts list against the box, and for RC, gather the electronics the airframe doesn't include: brushless motor, ESC, servos, and propellers — sold as bundles in the parts results.
    3. Build flat, in the plan's order.Pin parts over the plan sheet, glue one section at a time, and let each joint cure fully before moving on. A warped wing built in a hurry never flies straight.
    4. Cover, balance, then test.Cover or paint, fit the electronics, and balance the plane at the center-of-gravity mark in the instructions. Do a range and control-direction check on the ground before the first flight.
    Packing an overnight backpack? Same idea applies in reverse: start with the “big three” (pack in the 40–65L range, shelter, sleep system), lay everything out before it goes in, put heavy items close to your back and centered, and do a test carry around the block before the trail. Search “backpack” on the listings page to compare packs by size and price.
